# JSLC and Conquer — player manual

## 1. Your first match

Build a base, protect its income, and use a mixed force to break the opposition.
The space laser gets the headline. The supply route keeps the army alive.

This manual distinguishes **current play** from **planned missions and rules**.
Available units and actions depend on the selected mode.
A catalogue entry can describe a future design; its presence does not guarantee a usable action.

1. Open the current JSLC and Conquer game.
2. Choose a local match against the computer for your first session.
3. Select your faction and an available battlefield.
4. Check the fuel setting before the match.
5. Find your headquarters, builder, nearby supplies, and safe approach routes.
6. Establish power and basic production.
7. Scout before you commit expensive units.

JSLC teaches combined arms and fleet support.
AoI rewards pressure, movement, and tunnel survival.
Penguin design favors powerful specialist forces, though the full shield-and-warp style is not complete in current play.

Production can also intercept number keys while its panel is open.

## 3. Phone controls

1. Use Select to choose your units.
2. Tap an enemy to order a formation attack.
3. Tap friendly units, neutral units, or terrain to move there.
4. Use Unselect Units when you want to clear the selection.
5. Use two fingers to pan or pinch the view.

The selection stays active across ordinary taps.
The Select area splits to expose Unselect Units while a selection exists.
A deliberate new selection or order-mode change can change that state.
An enemy tap receives a larger hit area unless nearby enemies overlap.
Mixed formations travel at the slowest selected speed.
Separate a fast scout from a slow armored column when speed matters.

Two-finger gestures are camera actions.
A one-finger terrain tap with units selected is an order, not a camera gesture.
The phone layout still needs independent device acceptance; these instructions reflect its current control logic.

## 4. Read the battlefield

Terrain controls which units can reach a destination.
Ships need navigable water; ground units need a safe route or a transport.
Aircraft can cross separated land areas but still need fuel and compatible recovery where those systems apply.

**Ground units sink in deep water. This is an intended mechanic.**
An unembarked tank does not become a boat because you issued a move order.
Transport cargo can also sink when its carrier is lost over deep water.
The wreck remains an obstacle.

1. Inspect the crossing before you send ground units through it.
2. Load an amphibious force into compatible transports.
3. Protect the transport until its cargo reaches safe ground.

Large ships use real relative scale.
Zoom out before you route a carrier through a confined approach.
The optional small-unit readability aid changes appearance, not range or collision rules.
An accepted order does not guarantee that a blocked unit can complete it.

## 5. Build, produce, and expand

1. Select a builder for construction.
2. Open Build.
3. Choose an available structure.
4. Place its full foundation on valid terrain.
5. Complete its prerequisites before you request advanced units.

The current interface includes Build, Arsenal, and a new seven-tab Production panel.
Production groups Structures, Defences, Infantry, Vehicles, Aircraft, Naval, and Support.
Each producer advances one queued unit at a time.
An expanded queue holds up to eight jobs.
Insufficient supply, missing technology, incompatible production, water access, or airbase capacity can block a request.
Low power can halve production speed.
A naval queue can wait for a safe launch position.

The first eligible producer becomes primary.
Double-click a factory, or long-press it on a phone, to request a new primary.
Selecting another compatible factory directs the next purchase there.
Shift-click requests up to five units, limited by supply and queue space.
Right-click a production card cancels its latest matching queued job.
Split queues directs purchases toward a shorter eligible queue.
In local play, right-click terrain with only factories selected to set their rally points.
Remote rally routing is not implemented in this panel.
Primary-selection gestures and the new panel still need live device acceptance.

Workers collect finite deposits and return their cargo to a compatible drop-off.
Keep the return route short and defended.
Current local play also has income beyond direct collection, with reduced income after nearby depletion.
Expansion still matters; a protected forward deposit can sustain the next production cycle.

## 6. Faction openings

These are practical starting sequences, not tested competitive build orders.
Map resources and starting forces can change the sequence.

| Faction | Opening | Next decision |
|---|---|---|
| JSLC | Build a Fusion Plant, then Ground Command. Add Supply Works as income permits. | Scout with a mixed screen; choose Research Directorate and the defense layer that addresses the threat. |
| AoI | Build a Generator Shed, then Training Courtyard and Resource Plot. | Apply early pressure; choose Covered Workshop, Concealed Block, or a Tunnel Node for the situation. |
| Penguins | Build Khaydarin Pylon, then Gateway Warp Conduit and ZPM Subspace Siphon. | Preserve expensive specialists and expand before the starting area becomes a trap. |

JSLC needs support around expensive armor and aircraft.
A defense line buys time; it does not collect the next deposit for you.

AoI benefits from cheap mobile groups and concealed routes.
Completed core Tunnel Nodes can preserve command after headquarters loss.
An exposed tunnel network still needs protection.

The Penguin theme promises strong shields and powered deployment fields.
Current basic units and bonuses do not deliver the whole intended technology tree.
Use the actions actually offered by the selected producer.

## 7. Fight, preserve veterans, and repair

1. Scout the target.
2. Keep artillery and support behind a screen.
3. Concentrate fire on a useful target.
4. Pull damaged expensive units out before the next volley.
5. Keep friendly units clear of an area attack.

Friendly and allied forces can take blast damage.
Automatic enemy selection does not protect allies inside the explosion.
Unit names describe roles; they do not guarantee an undocumented damage bonus.

Core projectile kills promote units at three and ten kills.
Those ranks improve health and damage; the core veteran path also regenerates health.
Some expanded damage paths still need consistent kill credit.
Commander rank exists, but the intended promotion-point spending menu is not complete.

Repair specialists can receive an on-screen repair assignment to a compatible damaged target.
Eligible tanks can instead attempt a limited crew patch.
They need eight seconds without damage or firing, followed by three seconds of preparation.
The crew can restore up to fifteen percent of maximum health at two percent per second.
Light damage permits a ninety-percent ceiling; moderate damage permits seventy percent.
Below thirty-five percent health, the tank needs a specialist.
The crew action has a sixty-second cooldown.
Movement, combat, or another conflicting order can interrupt the work.

## 8. Patrol and escort

1. Select the force that will patrol.
2. Press P or use the patrol action.
3. Choose the patrol center and the radius requested by the interface.

A friendly or neutral unit can serve as a moving center.
The patrol follows that unit rather than remaining at its old position.

1. Select the escorting units.
2. Use the on-screen escort action.
3. Choose the friendly unit to protect.

Aircraft, ships, and ground escorts use different formation positions.
A teammate's unit can serve as the target in a team match.
Escort does not transfer ownership of your units.
Move, attack, stop, or construction orders replace the continuing assignment.
Route obstructions can still prevent a unit from reaching its assigned position.

## 9. PR and protected actors

The visible political meters are not yet a complete implementation of the intended rules below.
This section explains the design so an unfinished rule does not become a false promise.

**Planned common rule:** JSLC legitimacy and AoI support run from zero to one hundred.
All gains share a ceiling of twenty-five per sixty seconds.
Penalties can exceed that ceiling.
Evidence improves confidence but does not remove a protected actor's status.

1. Identify a target before you use lethal fire.
2. Keep blast areas clear of protected actors.
3. Use reconnaissance to expose a hijack or a hidden cache.
4. Use a nonlethal action when the scenario provides one.
5. Watch the incident reason and any rebuttal window.

Protected convoy trucks can carry hidden arms, but their hostage drivers remain protected after hijack.
The intended responses include ISR, disabling, boarding, and recovery.
A confirmed cache beneath a protected building reduces the penalty; it does not make demolition politically free.

The flotilla is the sharpest example.
The first JSLC lethal hit is enough to collapse legitimacy to zero under the contract.
Waiting for a sinking is too late.
LRAD disperses ordinary boats; water cannon also displaces them.
The flagship requires boarding and towing for permanent clearance before timeout.
These complete flotilla interactions are not established in the current active match.

Lawfare's contract includes warnings, condemnation, rebuttal, and a temporary terrestrial offensive freeze.
A later design revision rejects weapon lockouts and instead requests economic friction.
That conflict remains unresolved; the normative contract still defines conformance.
Do not treat zero legitimacy as a universal defeat condition in every mode.

## 10. Use the fleet

The carrier, LHD, destroyer, submarine, and corvette are different tools.
The current Ford-class INS Adir operates compatible carrier aircraft.
The Wasp LHD combines aviation with two included LCACs.
Burke and Sa'ar platforms have smaller aviation provisions.
Default air groups are compressed gameplay complements, not full real-world wings.

1. Select the aviation ship.
2. Inspect its carried craft and deck capacity.
3. Launch a compatible aircraft with the ship's available action.
4. Recover that aircraft before fuel becomes critical.

Auto complement replaces lost default craft when supply, technology, queues, and capacity allow.
It does not create a second copy of an aircraft still alive on patrol.

For a landing, stage actual cargo in the LCACs.
Use the available launch or Beach assault action after you inspect the coast.
The craft leave and return through the stern well deck.
Deploy ground units onto safe land.
Keep air defense and naval fire support near the transport route.

A Wasp-class LHD has a well deck; an America-class LHA-6 does not.
The “Guardian Carrier” catalogue identity is unresolved; do not assume it names a usable naval carrier.

Supply and repair ships support repeated operations where the selected platform exposes those services.
Watch fuel, ammunition, missiles, and repair state instead of assuming endless endurance.
The older guide's blanket statement that fuel is absent is stale.

## 11. Aircraft and fuel

The host chooses 1×, 2×, 5×, 100×, or infinite fuel endurance before the match.
Larger finite settings increase fuel capacity.
Infinite disables fuel burn.

Expanded aircraft need a compatible home base with an available slot.
A helicopter pad does not grant CATOBAR capability.
Some aircraft need a land airbase; others can use suitable carrier decks.
The current legacy roster still needs complete coverage of these rules.

1. Check basing compatibility before you buy the aircraft.
2. Watch its fuel gauge during the sortie.
3. Preserve a safe recovery route.
4. Protect an alternate compatible base when possible.

Airborne fixed-wing aircraft keep moving, including during loiter.
Rotary aircraft can hover, but hover still consumes fuel.
Afterburner consumes fuel faster.
Bingo fuel can override your order and send the aircraft home.
Destruction of its home base can force a diversion.
No compatible recovery option can leave the aircraft unable to avoid a fuel crash.
Current crash handling charges the aircraft's own team.

## 12. The orbital laser and other powers

1. Open the available strategic actions.
2. Check the cost, rank, prerequisite, and cooldown.
3. Inspect the target area.
4. Confirm the strike only after friendly and protected actors are clear.

The intended orbital rule exempts these powers from terrestrial lawfare restrictions.
It does not exempt them from protected-target consequences.
The full jurisdiction rule is not yet established in the active power system.

Current local JSLC skirmish charges the laser before you arm it and choose a target.
The base charge takes fifteen seconds.
Below forty legitimacy, charging takes longer, up to 37.5 seconds at zero.
The timer does not fire automatically or grant command resources.
A strike restarts the charge.
The dedicated charge display does not apply to authored campaign missions or remote matches.

## 14. Skirmish, tower defense, and multiplayer

The authored catalogue includes three 1v1 choices per faction and three maps each for four, six, and eight players.
Map entries alone do not guarantee launch support in every mode.
The three tower-defense maps still need a complete active-mode integration.

For multiplayer, use the match interface to join or create a room.
Choose teams and settings before all players ready up.
Each player keeps a separate base and army in team play.
Friendly blast damage still matters.

The match service must be available; the static game page alone cannot host the authority.
Current rooms do not provide durable server-restart recovery.
A page reload can lose session credentials.
Live multiplayer operation was not verified for this manual.

## 15. When an order appears to fail

| What you see | What to check |
|---|---|
| Unit purchase is blocked | Supply, research, compatible producer, queue space, and aircraft base capacity. |
| Construction refuses placement | The complete foundation, water, bridges, crossings, and nearby obstructions. |
| Production is slow | Power supply and the active scenario's stated effects. |
| Ship waits in production | Water access and room for the full hull at launch. |
| Aircraft ignores a new target | Bingo fuel, return-to-base state, home-base loss, or an incompatible action. |
| Fixed-wing aircraft circles | Airborne aircraft must keep moving; loiter is expected. |
| A ground unit disappears in water | Deep-water sinking is intentional; use a safe route or transport. |
| Escort or patrol does not advance | Route obstruction, terrain class, or a replaced order. |
| Tank crew repair stops | New damage, firing, movement, cooldown, or damage too severe for the crew. |
| Campaign map cannot launch | The catalogue can contain authored content without supported terrain or progression. |
| Named ability is absent | The catalogue entry may be a reference design rather than a current action. |

Free the chickies. Do not kill them.
The current local rescue uses proximity, while the intended guarded rescue remains incomplete.
Keep that distinction in mind when a scenario ends sooner than its fiction suggests.
